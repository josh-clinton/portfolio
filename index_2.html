What I'm actually about

I'm hooked on the exact moment an AI model stops being an experiment and starts changing something real. At B. Braun, that moment was watching my vision system flag a mislabeled vial that a person had missed — software quietly standing between a mistake and a patient.

I'm a CS student at Penn State, graduating December 2026, and I'm very passionate about learning the use cases for computer vision and applied machine learning — from the medical-vision system above to training the models behind AI coding tools at Handshake AI. I've also got a real interest in computer engineering — my time at Northrop Grumman writing C++ for FPGA-based hardware has pushed me past CS into embedded systems, and given me a better feel for how software actually has to behave inside a real product.

I'm a self-driven learner, and I'm always eager to pick up something new — a new corner of AI, a language I haven't touched, or a problem I don't yet know how to solve. I'm just as passionate about training my own brain as I am about training a neural network.
